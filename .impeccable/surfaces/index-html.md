# Surface brief: index.html

Scope: the whole tool, one screen. Visitor mode: **Operate**.

Audience: a trail runner building a fuelling plan at home on a computer the week
before a race, road-book to hand, and reading it again on a phone in the start
pen. Job: know how long each leg takes and what to carry for it, and know which
leg decides the pack. Content: the runner's own GPX, their flat pace, optional
typed aid stations, an optional known finishing time. Constraints: static, no
framework, no network call after load, GitHub Pages, tests run before deploy.

## Direction contract

THESIS: this page is the road-book the organiser did not print for you, and its
unit is the leg between two aid stations. It refuses the fitness dashboard, the
grid of same-size stat cards and the rounded panel: a road-book is ruled, not
boxed, and its numbers sit in a table because a table is what you read at 4 a.m.

OWN-WORLD: a two-ink print job. Near-black text ink plus one printer's green,
on stock, with a night edition that inverts both onto true black for a start pen
under a headtorch. Rules carry every division, never borders or cards: a heavy
rule opens a section, a hairline separates rows, a double rule closes a table.
One family, Archivo variable, worked through its width axis: condensed caps for
furniture and column heads, expanded for the one big figure, regular for prose,
tabular lining figures wherever a number is a measurement. A third restricted
system, the measurement ramp, lives only inside the profile band and its legend:
cold for descent, warm for climb, each band also hatched so no reading depends on
hue. Form fields are ruled baselines with a small-caps label beneath, the way a
paper form asks a question. A cut rule and its scissors mark are structural, not
ornament: they divide the document from the part you take with you.

STORY: a stranger understands in one screen that this turns their race file into
a per-leg plan, believes it because every number carries its range, its sample
size and its validity domain in the same breath, and acts by dropping a GPX in.
They leave with the pocket card.

FIRST VIEWPORT: a running head, ravito at left and carnet de route at right in
condensed caps over a 3px rule. Under it, at full measure, the blank booklet:
the profile band and the leg table printed as a ghost at low opacity, with the
file control ruled across it as the page's largest element and its only primary
action. The four inputs sit above it on one ruled line as form fields. With a
file loaded the ghost is replaced in place: the profile band spans the full
measure at 220px with triangle aid markers dropping onto a kilometre rule, the
estimate follows in expanded figures with its range on the same baseline, then
the leg table. Signature interaction: the pocket card. A cut rule runs under the
plan; taking it detaches a narrow strip carrying only R-number, kilometre, time
and grams, which is the phone layout and the print layout, one artifact.

FORM: the road-book, candidate 2 of the grounded list and the user's own choice
over the dealt assignment, seed key e76ea3d0.

Raises taken from the dealt hand, each a discipline and never a borrowed look:
- colour is never the sole signal, so every gradient band carries a hatch and a
  label (from the cyclorama challenger, declined);
- state is drawn by rule weight and a tick rather than a colour swap, and every
  leg keeps a durable R-number anchor (from the normalled jackfield, competitive);
- the profile band never wraps, it scales, and the empty state is a drawn
  invitation rather than a dashed box (from the step row, declined);
- every part of the drawing is labelled in place at constant scale instead of in
  a separate legend (from the cutaway spread, declined);
- a strict numbered margin column down the plan (from the fold sequence, declined);
- density steps are declared: the table drops named columns at named widths
  rather than squeezing (from the info-noise sleeve, declined).

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance.
